# Whiteboarding Runthrough 3

> **Prompt:** design a landing page for a physical product that costs more than $200

Session note: Claude acts as a collaborator.

## Purpose

> User need: **The mattress is expensive and hard to return, so customers need a lot of convincing before they commit.**

> Company need: **Raise conversion and prevent returns.**

> In this session, we hope to **design the landing page. The purchase steps are out of scope for now; we can come back to them if they matter.**

### Context Claude chose

- Product: a $1,200 hybrid mattress sold online by a direct-to-consumer brand. It is expensive, bulky and painful to return, and buyers can't try it before committing, which matches all of Helen's conditions.

### Ideas Helen raised (not yet decisions)

- Be very clear about what the product is and its benefits, so the wrong buyers don't purchase and return.
- Show how happy existing customers are.
- Show what makes it different: it solves problems competitors can't.
- Justify the price two ways: logically (it solves your problem) and through values (sustainability, fair wages; "you're supporting a good company").

## Person

People Helen named:

- Social media shoppers: easily influenced when they see a product that looks good.
- Health- or environmentally-conscious shoppers: cautious about what they let into their home.
- People with a physical sleep need: bad back pain, side sleepers, or anyone who sleeps a particular way.

> We are designing for **social media shoppers** because **they are easily convinced by other people's reviews, but have a short attention span, so the page has to catch them fast.**

- Why first (Helen): they shop a lot, leave reviews and post about products, so they bring free marketing. A good product for health/eco-conscious shoppers and people with a physical need should follow as a waterfall effect (including doctors recommending it).
- Business benefit: a high number of reviews and more publicity.

## Present

### Context Claude chose

- The brand already has a landing page. It was written for comparison shoppers who arrive from search, so it leads with specs and layer diagrams. Shoppers who tap through from social posts and ads leave quickly; it converts under 1% from social traffic, and about 1 in 8 mattresses come back during the trial.

> **A social shopper taps through from a post or ad → lands on the spec-led page → leaves fast, or buys without knowing if it fits and returns it.**

## Problem

- **The person needs:** to quickly understand whether this mattress is right for someone like them, and to be convinced it's worth the purchase. (Helen)
- **The pain points are:** (Helen)
  - The page doesn't grab attention, so shoppers leave fast.
  - It isn't clear what kind of person the mattress is a good fit for.
  - It isn't descriptive enough to convey what the mattress is actually like.
  - It doesn't convince them it's a good purchase.
- Helen's read on the cause: the information hierarchy, or the type of information shown.
- **We assume:** the mattress is a good product for certain types of people. (Helen)
- **We must work within:** we can't change the mattress itself. (Helen)
- Priority pain point: **conviction**, with fit addressed too, since a landing page can carry both. Reason (Helen): at $1,200 they'll think they could find a better option for cheaper.

### Problem statement

> When **a social media shopper taps through from a post to a $1,200 mattress page**, they want to **see quickly why it's worth more than cheaper options, and whether it suits how they sleep**, so they can **buy with confidence instead of leaving or sending it back**.

> Business benefit: **higher conversion from social traffic, fewer returns, and more reviews and publicity.**

## Scope

- Out of scope: deciding the mattress's actual features.
- In scope: the general landing page layout, and what makes a hybrid mattress better than other types of mattresses.

## Possibilities

Requirements Helen set: the page must be fast and attention-grabbing.

### Product facts (context Claude chose, at Helen's request)

- 12" hybrid, medium-firm (6/10).
- Layers: organic cotton cover → 2" natural latex comfort layer → wool fire barrier (no chemical flame retardants, no fiberglass) → 8" individually wrapped pocket coils, zoned firmer under the lower back and hips → recycled-steel base.
- Certifications: GOTS organic cotton, GOLS organic latex, GREENGUARD Gold low emissions.
- Benefits most: back and combination sleepers with lower back pain (zoned support); hot sleepers (latex and coils breathe, so it sleeps cooler than all-foam); couples (pocket coils limit motion transfer); health- and eco-conscious buyers (certified natural materials).
- Not ideal for: strict side sleepers who want a plush feel, or sleepers under about 130 lb, who may find it too firm.

### Options Helen raised

| # | Option | Impact | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hero with the mattress image, built to grab attention fast | High | Low |
| 2 | Unique features near the top | High | Low |
| 3 | "Who it's for" callout | High (fit, fewer returns) | Low |
| 4 | Customer reviews, so shoppers can imagine owning it | High (social shoppers trust reviews) | Low |
| 5 | Reviews from reputable sources | Medium–High | Low |
| 6 | Side-by-side with a popular mattress | High (answers "cheaper elsewhere") | Medium |
| 7 | Competitor comparison by feature | Medium (overlaps with #6) | Medium |
| 8 | Spec comparison: thickness, softness/firmness, how warm it gets | Medium (fit) | Low |
| 9 | GIFs of firmness and the layer technology | Medium | High (asset production) |
| 10 | Scientific research behind the benefits | Medium (stronger for health-conscious than social shoppers) | Medium |
| 11 | Sustainability, health and values stats (incl. fair wages, from Purpose) | Medium | Low |

Impact weighs value to social shoppers, progress on conviction and fit, and business benefit (conversion, returns). Ratings are Claude's first pass, not decisions.

Chosen direction (Helen): do everything except the costlier, less direct quadrant.

- In: 1 hero, 2 unique features, 3 who it's for, 4 customer reviews, 5 reputable-source reviews, 6 side-by-side with a popular mattress, 8 spec comparison, 11 sustainability/health/values stats.
- Out: 7 competitor feature table (overlaps with 6), 9 firmness and layer GIFs, 10 scientific research.

## Prototype & Proof

### Outline (Helen's order)

1. Hero image
2. Reputable-source reviews
3. Customer reviews (a little below)
4. Who it's for
5. Unique features
6. Thickness, firmness, warmth
7. Sustainability, health and values
8. Side-by-side with a popular mattress

Helen asked whether anything should be reordered.

### Claude's suggested reorder (suggestion, not decided)

1. Hero: image, one-line promise, price, star rating with review count, a reputable-source quote strip, primary CTA
2. Who it's for
3. Unique features
4. Side-by-side with a popular mattress
5. Customer reviews
6. Thickness, firmness, warmth
7. Sustainability, health and values
8. Closing CTA

Reasons: social shoppers decide fast, so the rating and press proof go in the hero; who it's for comes early so the wrong buyers self-select out (fewer returns); the side-by-side answers the priority pain ("cheaper elsewhere"), so it moves up from last; a closing CTA gives the page a clear end.

### Revised order (Helen)

1. Hero, with a purchase CTA (shoppers returning later shouldn't have to scroll to the bottom)
2. Unique features (what catches attention, so people stay longer)
3. Reputable-source reviews
4. Customer reviews
5. Who it's for
6. Thickness, firmness, warmth
7. Sustainability, health and values
8. Side-by-side with a popular mattress
9. Closing CTA

- Assumption (Helen): this is the only product on the website.

### Proof (Helen)

- Primary: higher conversion rate from this page.
- Attention signal: more time on the page, even when the mattress turns out not to be the right choice.
- Business: higher revenue from this page.
- Guardrail (from the company need): return rate shouldn't rise.

## Confirmed

- Product features are out of scope; focus on layout and why hybrid beats other mattress types
- Primary user: social media shoppers
- Current path and pain points (Helen)
- Constraint: the mattress can't change; assume it's good for certain types of people
- Priority: conviction first, fit too
- Direction: options 1–6, 8, 11
